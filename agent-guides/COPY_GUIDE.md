# AICC Copy Guide

Read `AICC_BRIEF.md` first. AICC shares the studio's brand voice with AISC (`../../aisc/agent-guides/COPY_GUIDE.md` is the parent document) but runs at a calmer register. This guide states what is inherited verbatim and what is different.

## Register: the AICC delta

AISC is a challenge; its copy has momentum and stakes. AICC is counsel; its copy has weight and calm. The reader is a Chief or VIP deciding whether to invite you into their world for four weeks.

- **Calm over urgency.** No countdowns, no scarcity pressure, no "seats are going fast." The 5-seats-per-month cap and 10-year eligibility line are stated once, as fact. The filter is a feature; it is never shouted.
- **"Engagement" or "program", never "challenge".** "Challenge" belongs to AISC.
- **Paced, not intense.** Never sell speed or intensity. Sell depth, privacy, and fit around the leader's calendar.
- **Counsel language.** Words that fit: briefing, counsel, session, mandate, alignment, governance, clarity, direction, discretion. Words that don't: bootcamp, sprint, hack, masterclass, accelerator.
- **Value = extraction.** Write about understanding, extracting, and exploiting AI value in the leader's domain. Never promise a "declared territory" — that is AISC's capstone.
- **Labs are optional depth.** "As hands-on as you want it to be" — never a requirement, never the headline.

## Inherited verbatim from the studio voice (apply fully)

**Brand voice:** a trusted advisor who has seen what the best and worst leaders do when faced with a major shift, telling the truth about it plainly. Not a marketer, not a hype merchant, not an academic. Clear, direct, simple, warm, credible, ambitious.

**Headline rules — every headline must pass all three:**
1. Can the audience visualize it? (Abstract states are not images.)
2. Can the audience falsify it? (Too vague to disprove = too vague to believe.)
3. Can they hear it somewhere else? (If yes, it belongs to no one.)

**Write for the full breadth of Chiefs.** A bishop with 50,000 members, a celebrity with 5 million followers, a politician with a constituency, and a CEO with 300 employees are all reading this page. Speak to what they share: the pressure of leading, the confusion around AI, and the expectation that they will have an answer.

**Assume low AI fluency.** Never assume the reader already uses AI. No "as you know with ChatGPT." This may be the first honest conversation they've had about AI.

**The Chief's internal monologue** (write to this): "I keep hearing about AI from everyone below me and everyone trying to sell me something. I've approved budgets for it. I've sat through briefings about it. I still don't have a clear position that I'd defend in front of the people I lead."

**Chief hooks that land:**
- Lead with what they already have: "You have the platform. The resources. The access. What you need is clarity on where AI fits in all of it."
- Speak to their exposure: "The people around you are making AI decisions with or without your direction."
- The followers angle: their congregation, audience, board, or voters are already asking what AI means — after AICC, the leader is the one with the answer, and it is theirs, not borrowed.

**Never, for Chiefs:** "stay relevant" (implies fragility), "learn AI" (they lead it), productivity as the main hook, or any suggestion they are behind.

**No AI jargon:** "LLMs," "prompting," "model," "tokens," "fine-tuning" are banned in rendered copy.

## CTA principles

- Primary CTA everywhere: **"APPLY TO SEE IF YOU QUALIFY"** (existing, keep — it is calm and filtering).
- Offer-level CTAs when needed: "Request Your Briefing" (Strategic Briefing), "Apply for the Full Program", "Convene Your Council".
- Never "Sign up", "Register", "Buy now", "Enroll today".
- Never apologize for the price. State it plainly next to what it buys. "Calm and certain. The price is what it is."
